
No other test of slice 1 noticed the grips: every press, click, drag, resize and marquee of the 470 lands where it

### 8. What surprised me

1. **A grip must not take the pointer while it is not shown.** My first `grips.css` hid a grip by `opacity: 0` and
   left it in the pointer's way, so that the pointer on a grip was on its node. Then a press on "Fuel's right grip"
   pressed **Signal's left grip**: the two boxes are 28 px apart, their grips lie on one another, and the later
   node's wins. And a part placed at the adapter's least distances has its left grip **on its owner's left border**
   (12 - 14 = 2 px outside it) and its top grip in its owner's header (48 - 14 = 34, in a header of 40), often **on
   the owner's name**. Now a grip is live only while shown (the pointer over its node, the node selected, a line in
   hand), and a shown grip also takes the pointer on the way between the node's side and itself, so that the pointer
   going from the node to the grip never leaves the node and the grips do not blink off in the 10 px between.
   What follows from it: the pointer comes to a grip **from its node**. Coming from outside, the place of a grip that
   is not shown is the ground (seen). And where a border item sits at a side's middle (29 of 115), the way out is
   under the border item: that side's grip is reached only while the box is selected, or one of the other three is
   used.
2. **The classes that are needed are `nokey` and `nodrag`; `nopan` changes nothing today.** Each taken off in the page
   (`.probe/classes.mjs`): without `nokey` and with Shift, the press never reaches the plane and a marquee starts;
   without `nodrag`, the press reaches the plane and the gesture hears everything, **and React Flow drags the node
   too**; without `nopan`, nothing changes, because React Flow gives a draggable node's element and every edge's
   `<g>` the class `nopan` itself. It stays on a grip for a node that is not draggable.
3. **While the pointer is captured, `:hover` does not move.** The grip's node stays `:hover` for the whole gesture
   (its thin outline stays, which reads as "the line starts here"), and the node under the pointer never is. So the
   outline of what a line is over cannot be CSS's; it is `body.mark`, as the plan has it. Seen: the other box gets
   `outline: dashed 2px` from `mark` while `:hover` is false on it.
4. **z-index, three ways.** (a) A selected line's grips, half under their nodes (answer 4). (b) An owner's grip can
   lie **under a line of its parts**: React Flow gives an edge the level of its deepest end, so `mount`, which starts
   at `Page.hosts.api`, lies over `Page.hosts`'s right grip, and a press there is on the line (12 px wide for the
   pointer). A grip cannot be raised out of its node's stacking context. (c) A grip can lie under a sibling node that
   comes later (`FuelPort.supply`'s bottom grip under `FuelPort.back`). In each case the other grips are free.
5. **`hitAt` answers with a line where a line crosses a box.** A line let go on a box exactly where a line runs over
   it is "on a line" by seam 2's rule, and flow 1 then says "Drop the line on the element it goes to." Package 2-2
   may want the first node, not the first node or edge, for a drop.
6. **The edge label renderer is not used.** A grip drawn through `EdgeLabelRenderer` or `ViewportPortal` is outside
   its edge's element, so the plane could not read whose it is from the document, and `EndGrips({ points })` has no
   id to write on it. Inside the edge's svg, `closest('.react-flow__edge')` gives the relationship and the two
   circles give `ends`. React Flow has `useNodeId()` and nothing like it for an edge.
7. **A border of 1.5 px is laid out as 1 px.** Chromium rounds a border's width down to whole device pixels, so a
   border item's padding box starts 1 px in at single density and 1.5 px in at double (the screenshots). Placed from
   the inside of the border, its grip was 14.5 px out. It is placed from the square's middle now, which no border
   moves. A box's border is 1 px and has no such trouble.
8. **The far view**: below 25% a node renders no grip (`NodeGrips` returns null on `far`), and the hover outline is
   off for `.far`. At 24% the box has no `[data-grip]` child. The pan tests of `scale.test.mjs` are all far.
9. **The middle button.** React Flow's filter lets a middle-button press on a node or an edge pan whatever classes
   it carries (`@xyflow/system`, `createFilter`, its first test). So when the scaffold gives `panOnDrag` the middle
   button, a middle press on a grip pans, as on a node. The plane hands only the main button to the gesture.
10. **React Flow swallowed no event of the gesture.** Not the press, no move, not the release, not the click.

## Built otherwise than the plan says, each with its reason

1. **`NodeGrips` takes a second, optional prop, `side`.** Seam 6 writes `NodeGrips({ far })`. With `far` alone the
   component cannot know which one grip a border item has. Called with `far` only it gives four, so the seam's call
   holds as written. To keep to the letter instead: mount four in every node and let `grips.css` hide three by the
   node's class, which needs the side as a class on a border item's node (the adapter gives none) and leaves three
   dead elements in each of them.
2. **A small node's grip is at the bottom.** "On its outer side" does not say which side for a node that is on no
   border.
3. **The plane drops the click after every press on a grip**, not only after a gesture's `swallow()`. Flow 1 says a
   release before 4 px "does nothing", and seam 6 has the gesture call `swallow()` past 4 px; with only that, a click
   on a grip is a click on its node, and with a tool in hand it places (answer 3). One line in `Plane.tsx`'s
   listener; take it out to have the seam's letter. `swallow()` is still what a pin's gesture needs.
4. **`EndGrips({ points })` takes the route's points**, the array `route()` gives; the first is the `from` end and the
   last the `to` end. The seam does not give `points` a type, and `GripPress.ends` is `{ from, to }`.
5. **A line's grips are circles inside the edge's own svg**, and so can be half under a node (answer 4). The seam says
   "at each drawn end" and no more. Whole grips need one of: `elevateEdgesOnSelect` on the plane, which I tried and
   which works, and which changes what a selected line lies over; or a portal, for which `EndGrips` needs the
   relationship's id as a prop.
6. **`drawsLines(session, viewId)` in `core/commands.ts`.** The plan gives the condition of `data-grips` and names no
   function for it, and `Plane.tsx` may not read a view's kind itself (rule 5).

Beside those, three choices the plan leaves open, taken in `grips.css`: a grip is in the view's units (answer 4); a
grip that is not shown takes no pointer, and a shown one has a way to it (answer 8); the line under the pointer gets a
soft teal band on its 12 px hit path, since a line has no outline.

## What I tried and dropped

- **A grip hidden by opacity and always live** (the first `grips.css`): the wrong grip pressed, and dead spots on an
  owner's name and border. Answer 8, the first point.
- **A grip live only while shown, with no way to it**: the grips blink off in the 10 px between the node's side and
  the grip, and the pointer cannot arrive. The way (a `::before` of the grip, 16 px wide, from the grip to the side)
  fixes it.
- **A ring around a hovered node that keeps it hovered** (thought through, not built): it is the node's own element,
  so a press on it would drag the node, and it would lie 20 px wide over the owner.
- **A click on a grip passed on to what lies under it** (thought through, not built): more than the plan asks.
- **`opacity` for showing**: about 2 ms more of each frame that shows a node's grips than `visibility`.
- **`elevateEdgesOnSelect`**: tried for the line's grips, works, taken out again: it is the lead's to decide.
- **A border item's grip placed from inside its border**: 14.5 px at single density.
