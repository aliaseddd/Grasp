## The answers

### 1. A press on a grip and a drag of 100 px

As the plan needs it. On the example (`Page`, right grip, then top grip) and on the interconnection fixture (`Fuel`):

- The press reaches the plane's capturing `pointerdown` listener, which builds the GripPress (`grip`, `element`) and
  calls `connectOf(store).press(press, body)`.
- The node does not move, at no step and at the end (every node's rectangle read before, in the middle and after).
- The plane does not pan (the viewport's transform read at the same three moments).
- `setPointerCapture` on the grip holds: `hasPointerCapture` is true at the press and still true at the `pointerup`,
  and no `lostpointercapture` comes before the release.
- The window hears every event to the end: the gesture's capturing listeners heard 10 `pointermove` of 10 and 1
  `pointerup`, and a test's own bubbling listeners on the window heard the same 10 and 1 (and 10 `mousemove`, 1
  `mouseup`): React Flow stops none of them, because d3's drag and zoom never start.
- `data-connecting` is on the root from the first move past 4 px until the release, and off after.

### 2. Shift, and Ctrl

As the plan needs it.

- **Shift held**: no marquee (`.react-flow__selection` is never in the document), the press reaches the plane, the
  gesture hears all 10 moves and the release. This is the class `nokey` at work: with `nokey` taken off the grips in
  the page and Shift held, the press does **not** reach the plane's listener and a marquee starts (React Flow's pane
  handles `pointerdown` in React's capture phase, which runs before a native capturing listener on the plane's root,
  and stops it).
- **Ctrl held**, with another box picked first: that box is still the one picked after the release, and the grip's box
  is not picked. From the picked box's own grip: it stays picked. Nothing is picked and nothing is let go.

### 3. The click after the release

As the plan needs it, with one line more than the seam's letter ("Built otherwise", 3).

- After a drag past 4 px, Chromium sends a `click`, and **its target is the grip** (the pointer is captured there), so
  through React Flow's node wrapper it is a click on the grip's node. `swallow()` raises the flag and `clicked()` drops
  it: the grip's node is not picked.
- With the part def tool in hand: nothing is placed (13 nodes before and after; 27 and 27), no toast, and the tool is
  still in hand.
- **A release before 4 px**: the gesture does nothing (no `state`, no `swallow()`, `data-connecting` never set). What
  the click then does depends on one line of `Plane.tsx`:
  - With the seam's letter only (the gesture calls `swallow()`, and it does so past 4 px), **the click picks the
    grip's node**, and **with a tool in hand it places an element in that node** (seen: `Page` picked; a part def
    made, 13 nodes to 14, the tool put down).
  - As the worktree has it now, the plane's listener raises the flag at every press on a grip: the click does nothing
    at all. Nothing picked, nothing placed, the tool stays in hand.

### 5. hitAt

As seam 2 describes it. With a line in hand and the pointer on the middle of a grip that lies over another node (the
topmost element there is that grip), `hitAt` answered with the node under it in 9 of 12 tries, and in the other 3 with
a line that crosses the node at that point, which is its rule ("the first that is a node or an edge"). Never the
grip's own node, never the ground. Example: the pointer on `Page.hosts`'s top grip gives `el:Page`; on
`FuelPort.back`'s top grip gives `el:FuelPort.supply`.

Such grips are common: 9 of the example's 40 grips lie over another node, 33 of the interconnection view's 78, mostly
a part's grip over its owner. That is why a grip that is not shown takes no pointer (answer 8, the first point).

### 7. Which existing tests fail with the grips mounted

`npm test` with the stand-in in, twice.

**The first full run** (the middle state of `grips.css`): 470 tests, 468 pass, **2 fail**, 0 todo, 342 s.

1. `scale.test.mjs` › "a drag step takes under 16 ms at 5,000 elements in 3 views": 19.9 ms starting (two samples,
   20.7 and 19.0), 11.0 following. The timed test that failed in the baseline's full run (17.3). By itself the file
   passed 3 of 3 before that run and 3 of 3 on the final build.
2. `tools.test.mjs` › "while a tool is in hand the plane shows a crosshair, and a click picks nothing by itself":
   "The first end Host has no dashed outline. 'solid' !== 'dashed'". **Mine, and real**: the pointer is still over
   `Host` after the click that took it as a line's first end, and my rule for the node under the pointer
   (`outline: 1px solid`) weighed more than the plane's rule for a first end (`outline: 2px dashed`). Fixed in
   `grips.css`: the hover rule is written inside `:where()`, so it weighs no more than `.react-flow__node:hover`,
   and any outline the plane gives a node for a reason wins (a first end; `body.mark`). The test is not touched.
   `tools.test.mjs` by itself afterwards: 14 of 14.
