# Step 11: the Tools view and the right-click palettes

This is the scaffold of step 11 of `plan/slice-1.md`. The contracts are the comments in the skeleton files it names; this file says how the plan's open points are settled, what tests and code must agree on, and what each test does. `plan/swarm/README.md` says how the tasks run, and `plan/swarm/shared.md` what the steps share.

## 1. What the step delivers

The plan's own sentences (`plan/slice-1.md`, section 5, step 11):

> **What it delivers.** One table of commands feeds both. The Tools view lists the tools of the active tab's view kind, in the groups its `tools` row gives. A click arms an element tool, and the next click on the plane places the element. A relationship tool takes two clicks, the first end and then the second. Escape puts a tool down. A right-click on the ground or on a box opens a menu of what can be made there: the element kinds the view admits and the box can hold, and on a box or a port the relationship kinds that may start there. What a rule refuses is said in a toast, in the rule's own sentence.
>
> **Done when.** `tools.test.mjs` passes. In each of the four graph view kinds, on the example and the fixtures, every tool of the kind's row is used once where the rules allow it: the element or line appears on the plane and in the Explorer, `M.validate` finds nothing new, and Undo takes it back. A port clicked onto the empty ground is refused, and the toast says where it can go. A specialization from a part def to a port def is refused in the library's sentence. Escape puts the tool down. `palette.png` shows what its row lists.

`palette.png`'s row in section 4: "The right-click menu on a part def, at the pointer, in its groups". Damon decided that relationships are drawn by two clicks in this slice (section 7, item 3); drawing by dragging from handles is slice 2's.

What the plan leaves open, and how it is settled:

1. **Where the logic lives.** All of it is in the core, `core/commands.ts`, which runs in Node: the table (`toolGroups`), what a command makes (`place`, `draw`), and the tool in hand as a state machine (`Tools`, `toolsOf(session)`). The Tools view, a plane and the palette hold no rule: the view calls `arm`, a plane calls `click`, the palette calls `offers` and `choose`, the toasts read `said`. Reason: the rules can then be tested without a browser, and the two ways to a command cannot disagree.
2. **The table is the library's row.** A view kind's tools are `VIEW_KINDS[kind].tools`, cut at `'|'`. The palette offers the same tools, filtered by the rules, not every kind a general view admits. Reason: the plan's "one table feeds both" and `palette.png`'s "in its groups"; slice 4 is where the direction gives a palette "every kind a view admits".
3. **A view no plane draws lists no tools.** A sequence view and a geometry view have tools in the library, but no plane draws them in slice 1, so `toolGroups` gives none and the Tools view says "A sequence view has no tools here yet. The old page draws it until slice 2." Reason: the plan's own, section 7: a listed tool that does nothing is worse than none.
4. **Each command is the old page's, rule for rule**, in one `history.edit()`: `place` (the graph renderer's, with the two wrappers), `freeName`, `pairProblem` and `createRel`. The words are the old page's: "Added part def PartDef1", "Drew App specializes Page". `commands.ts`'s comments give every rule and number.
5. **A refusal keeps the tool in hand**, as on the old page, and says why in a toast. A relationship rule's sentence is followed by " Escape puts the tool down.", as on the old page, since the tool is still in hand; a placing rule's sentence stands alone.
6. **What a tool makes is picked, and nothing is said of it.** The old page said "Added … to view …. Name it in the panel." and put the focus into the name field. Here the element is picked, so Details shows its name field (step 10), Undo's tooltip names the step, and no toast is shown. The focus is not moved: naming in place is slice 2's.
7. **What puts a tool down**: what it made; Escape; a click on its own button; a click on the ground or on a line with a relationship tool; the view in front changing to one whose row lacks the tool, or to none; a click in such a view. An undo, a redo, a drag and a change in Details leave it in hand, as on the old page; only a first end whose element left the model is let go.
8. **A drag with a tool in hand is still a drag.** On the old page a tool in hand made boxes immovable. Here a press that moves 4 px or more drags the box or pans the ground as always, and only a click is the tool's. Reason: React Flow tells a click from a drag by distance already (step 7), and switching every node's `draggable` when a tool is armed would render every node of a 5,000-element view.
9. **What the plane shows while a tool is in hand**: a crosshair for a pointer, and a dashed teal outline on the first end of the line being drawn. The plane's root carries `data-tool` and `data-first` for the tests.
10. **Escape** is heard on the window, in the capture phase, by the Tools panel, which is mounted from the page's start. It acts from anywhere but a text field (there the key is the field's, as on the old page and as the bar's Undo), and not behind a modal dialog.
11. **The palette always opens** on a right-click on the ground, a node or a line. Where nothing can be made (a line, a done node) it holds one disabled entry, "Nothing can be made here." Reason: a right-click that sometimes does nothing reads as broken, and slice 2 adds entries (Delete, Remove from view) to this menu. A right-click picks nothing and is not a click for the tool in hand.
12. **The palette is no modal dialog.** The bar's keys act while it is open, and it closes when the model changes under it. One Escape closes it and puts a tool in hand down.
13. **The toasts** show one sentence at a time, at the bottom of the window, for 8 s, with role `status`. Only the tools speak through them in slice 1.
14. **"`M.validate` finds nothing new" cannot hold for three tools**, because a new thing of their kind always lacks something: a new interface def has no ends, a new initial node has no transition, and a new flow between ports has no item. The tests ask for exactly that one sentence and nothing else new (section 4, the table of uses). This departs from the letter of "Done when" and is in what the scaffold's author sent back to the lead.
15. **The notes** are not touched when a second definition replaces a typing line (`draw`'s comment says why and what the old page then shows).

### The plane's part

`plane/Plane.tsx` is changed by step 9 first, so its skeleton cannot hold step 11's part in full. This is the contract `11-impl-plane` builds, and the text that stands as the last item of `Plane`'s contract comment ("Step 11, the tool in hand and the palette"):

- **The tool before the pick.** `clicked(hit)`, the one function every click on the plane goes through (step 9), offers the click to the tool first: `if (!toolsOf(session).click(hit)) selectionOf(session).click(hit)`. Nothing else of step 9's picking changes: a marquee and its picks are as they were, with or without a tool in hand.
- **While a tool is in hand** (`toolsOf(session).armed`, read through `useSyncExternalStore(tools.listen, ...)`), the plane's root carries `data-tool`, the tool's kind, and has no such attribute otherwise. `plane/palette.css` makes the pointer a crosshair over the ground, the nodes and the lines of a root that carries it. Nothing else changes: a press that moves is still a drag of a box or a pan of the ground, the resize handles work, and only a click is the tool's.
- **While a line's first end is taken** (`tools.first` is not null), the root carries `data-first`, that element's id, and holds one `<style>` element with one rule, which gives the node of that element on this plane a dashed outline in the theme's teal: `[data-testid="plane"][data-view="<view id>"] .react-flow__node[data-id="<element id>"] { outline: 2px dashed var(--grasp-teal); outline-offset: 3px; }`. The ids are the library's (letters, digits, `_`, `.`, `:`, `-`), so they stand between the quotes as they are. With no first end there is no attribute and no `<style>`. Every plane of the session that draws the element marks it.
- **Reading the tools renders nothing more on a pan or a drag step**: the plane renders again only when `armed` or `first` changes.
- **A right-click.** The root's own `onContextMenu` prevents the browser's menu for every right-click inside it: on the ground, a node, a line, the zoom buttons, the minimap, a marquee's rectangle, and the card of a view no plane draws. React Flow's `onPaneContextMenu`, `onNodeContextMenu` and `onEdgeContextMenu` open the palette for the ground, a node (wherever in it, its name outside it included) and a line: with the Hit of that right-click, `{ view, type: 'ground' | 'el' | 'rel', id, at: screenToFlowPosition({ x: clientX, y: clientY }), add: false }`, and the pointer's `clientX`, `clientY` as `left`, `top`. A right-click anywhere else on the plane opens nothing. A right-click picks nothing, is no click for the tool in hand, and starts no drag.
- **The palette** is one `<Palette session at onClose />` (`plane/Palette.tsx`) for the plane, `at` held in the plane's state: null until a right-click sets it, and null again when `onClose` is called. A plane whose tab is hidden is not mounted, so its palette is closed with it.
- **`plane/viewStore.ts` is not changed by step 11.**

### `canvas/app/test/commands.test.mjs` (Node)

## 6. Decisions

Where the plan is silent, each with its reason. Items 1 to 15 of section 1 are the main ones; these are the rest.

- **`arm` does not ask which view is in front; the click does.** The Tools view only offers the row's tools, and a palette's choice comes with its own view.
- **Arming, and everything else the tools do, clears what was said.** A toast is about the last thing the tool did, so a stale refusal never stands beside a new state.
- **A toast is not closed by a click elsewhere.** The next click is usually the tool's, and what the tool says of it replaces the toast.
- **`data-testid="toast"` and role `status` are on the Snackbar's content**, whose text is the sentence. Material UI's default role there is `alert`, which the page keeps for a file that cannot be opened; Dockview has an empty `alert` region of its own (`plan/notes.md`, session 18).
- **`data-testid="palette"` is on the element with role `menu`.** A `data-testid` given to the Menu itself lands on its root, which covers the window (read in `@mui/material/Menu/Menu.js`, and seen in a probe).
- **The menu and the toast have no transition.** A test then reads a place and an absence at once, and the shot needs no wait.
- **The first end's mark is one `<style>` rule in the plane's root**, naming the node by its `data-id`. The store is not changed for it: step 11 leaves `viewStore.ts` alone, and the mark costs nothing while no line is being drawn.
- **A right-click on the open menu or beside it only closes it.** Opening it again at the new point in one gesture left the focus outside the menu in a probe, and Escape then did not close it.
- **The Tools view's buttons are plain buttons in the tab order**, with no arrow-key moving: a toolbar's roving focus is more than a list of at most 13 needs.
- **The Tools view shows the view's title above the list**, since with two groups of tabs side by side nothing else says whose tools these are.
- **The commands read the library through `CommandsLibrary` and `libOf`**, not through more members of the global `SysMLModelLibrary`, so that no two steps' files declare one member twice.

Tried and dropped:

- **The first end marked through the view's store**, as step 9 marks what is selected: it would have made step 11 change `viewStore.ts` for one node's class.
- **`M.validate` asked through `window.Grasp`**: a test may not read it. The browser tests save the file through the bar and validate the download in Node.
- **One browser test file for the whole step**: three writers could not have worked at once, and the panel's builder would have had no test to run before the planes take a tool.
- **Closing a toast on `clickaway`**: the click that follows a refusal would have taken away the sentence before it was read when that click went to a panel.
